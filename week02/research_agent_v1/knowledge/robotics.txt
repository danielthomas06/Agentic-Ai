Visual odometry estimates a camera's motion by analyzing consecutive images.

Feature-based visual odometry detects distinctive image features and tracks them across frames.

Optical flow can also be used to estimate motion between consecutive frames.

Visual odometry is commonly used in robotics, drones, autonomous vehicles, and augmented reality.

GPS-denied navigation systems can combine visual odometry with inertial measurements to estimate vehicle motion when GPS is unavailable.

SLAM extends visual odometry by simultaneously estimating the robot's trajectory and building a map of the environment.
